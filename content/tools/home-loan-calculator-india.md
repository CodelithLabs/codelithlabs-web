## 🔧 Technical Details

### Processing Technology
- **EMI Formula**: EMI = [P × r × (1 + r)^n]/ [(1 + r)^n - 1]
  - P = Principal loan amount
  - r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12 / 100)
  - n = Loan tenure in months
- **Tax Benefit on Principal**: Min(Principal repaid in year, Rs. 1.5 lakh) × Your tax slab
- **Tax Benefit on Interest**: Min(Interest paid in year, Rs. 2 lakh) × Your tax slab (for self-occupied property)
- **Outstanding Principal** = Reduces every month as per amortization schedule

## 📝 Best Practices

1. **Maximize down payment to lower EMI burden** — Every Rs. 1 lakh extra in down payment reduces your loan by Rs. 1 lakh, saving approximately Rs. 2 lakh in interest over 20 years (at 9% rate). Aim for 30-40% down payment if possible.
2. **Choose tenure carefully** — Longer tenure (25-30 years) keeps EMI low but increases total interest dramatically. A Rs. 50 lakh loan at 9% for 30 years costs Rs. 93 lakh in interest vs. Rs. 32 lakh for 15 years. Balance EMI affordability with total cost.
3. **Make annual prepayments using bonus/windfall** — Prepaying even Rs. 1 lakh per year can reduce a 20-year loan by 5-6 years and save lakhs in interest. Most banks allow prepayment up to 25% of principal per year without penalty.
4. **Claim full tax benefits** — Home loan interest up to Rs. 2 lakh is deductible under Section 24(b) and principal repayment up to Rs. 1.5 lakh under Section 80C. If you're in 30% slab, this translates to Rs. 1.05 lakh annual tax savings.
5. **Consider floating rate for long tenures** — Fixed-rate home loans are typically 0.5-1% higher than floating rates. For 15-20 year loans, floating rates are usually better since you benefit if RBI cuts rates, and India's long-term inflation trend is downward.
6. **Check hidden costs** — Include processing fees (0.25-1% of loan), legal charges, property valuation fees, insurance premiums, and prepayment penalties when comparing bank offers. A 8.5% loan with high fees may cost more than a 8.75% loan with nil charges.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### How much home loan can I get on Rs. 1 lakh salary?
Banks typically approve home loans where EMI is 40-50% of net monthly income (after deducting existing EMIs). On Rs. 1 lakh salary, you can afford Rs. 40,000-50,000 EMI. At 9% interest for 20 years, Rs. 45,000 EMI corresponds to a loan of approximately Rs. 50 lakh. Adding 20% down payment (Rs. 12.5 lakh), you can afford a property worth Rs. 62.5 lakh. However, actual eligibility depends on your age, employment stability, CIBIL score, and existing liabilities.

### What is the tax benefit on home loan?
You get two tax benefits:  
(a) **Section 80C**: Principal repayment up to Rs. 1.5 lakh per year is deductible from taxable income.  
(b) **Section 24(b)**: Home loan interest up to Rs. 2 lakh per year is deductible (for self-occupied property).  
If you're in the 30% tax bracket and pay Rs. 1.5 lakh principal + Rs. 2 lakh interest per year, your tax saving is (Rs. 3.5 lakh × 30%) = Rs. 1.05 lakh per year. For rented-out property, the entire interest (no Rs. 2 lakh cap) is deductible under Section 24(b).

### Should I choose fixed or floating interest rate?
Floating rates are typically 0.5-1% lower than fixed rates and benefit from RBI rate cuts. Fixed rates offer EMI certainty for the entire tenure. For long-term loans (15+ years), floating rates are usually better since India's average inflation and interest rates have been declining over decades. For short tenures (5-7 years) or if you expect interest rates to rise sharply, fixed may be safer. You can also opt for partially fixed (fixed for first 3-5 years, then floating).
