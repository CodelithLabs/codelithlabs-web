# Sleep Cycle Calculator

Find the optimal time to go to bed or set your alarm based on how sleep cycles work. A complete sleep cycle lasts approximately 90 minutes, and waking up at the end of a cycle (during light sleep) leaves you feeling refreshed, while waking in the middle of a cycle (during deep sleep) causes grogginess. This calculator helps you align your sleep and wake times with natural sleep cycles so you can wake up feeling alert and energized, even if you cannot get the full recommended eight hours.

## 🚀 Features

- **Bedtime Suggestions** — Enter your desired wake-up time and get a list of optimal bedtimes aligned to 90-minute sleep cycles
- **Wake Time Suggestions** — Enter when you plan to go to sleep and see the best alarm times to wake at the end of a cycle
- **Sleep-Now Mode** — Click "I'm going to sleep now" to instantly see the best alarm times based on the current time
- **Fall-Asleep Buffer** — Accounts for the average 15-minute time it takes to fall asleep, so recommendations are based on actual sleep onset, not just when you get into bed
- **Cycle Count Display** — Each suggested time shows how many complete 90-minute cycles you will get (4, 5, or 6 cycles)
- **Sleep Quality Indicator** — Color-coded recommendations showing which options provide ideal sleep duration (7-9 hours) versus shorter options
- **Nap Calculator** — Separate mode for calculating ideal nap durations (20-minute power nap or 90-minute full cycle nap)

## 📖 How to Use Sleep Cycle Calculator

1. **Choose Your Mode** — Select "I need to wake up at..." if you have a fixed alarm time, or "I plan to sleep at..." if you have a fixed bedtime, or "I'm going to sleep now" for instant recommendations.
2. **Enter Your Time** — Type or select the relevant time using the time picker.
3. **Adjust Fall-Asleep Time** — The default is 15 minutes. If you know you typically take longer or shorter to fall asleep, adjust this value.
4. **Review Suggestions** — The calculator presents multiple options, each showing the number of complete sleep cycles and total sleep duration.
5. **Pick the Best Option** — Choose the time that gives you 5 or 6 complete cycles (7.5 or 9 hours) if possible; 4 cycles (6 hours) works for occasional short nights.
6. **Set Your Alarm** — Use the recommended time to set your alarm or plan your bedtime accordingly.

### Travel and Jet Lag Management
When traveling across time zones, aligning your new sleep schedule to full cycles helps your body adjust faster. Enter your planned sleep time in the destination time zone to find the best wake time, helping you establish a healthy rhythm more quickly.

## 🔧 Technical Details

### Processing Technology
- Each sleep cycle is modeled as 90 minutes (the average length documented in sleep research spanning light sleep, deep sleep, and REM stages)
- Fall-asleep latency (default 15 minutes) is added to the sleep onset time
- Bedtime suggestions: Wake time minus (N x 90 minutes) minus fall-asleep buffer, for N = 6, 5, 4, and 3 cycles
- Wake time suggestions: Sleep onset time plus fall-asleep buffer plus (N x 90 minutes), for N = 3, 4, 5, and 6 cycles
- All computation runs in client-side JavaScript using standard time arithmetic

### Supported Formats
- Inputs: Time in 12-hour (AM/PM) or 24-hour format, fall-asleep duration in minutes
- Outputs: List of recommended times with cycle count and total sleep duration

- Works on all modern browsers including mobile

## 📝 Best Practices

1. **Aim for 5 or 6 complete cycles** — Five cycles (7.5 hours) is the sweet spot for most adults. Six cycles (9 hours) is ideal when recovery from sleep debt is needed. Avoid regularly relying on just 3 or 4 cycles.
2. **Keep a consistent sleep schedule** — Going to bed and waking up at the same time every day (including weekends) reinforces your circadian rhythm and improves sleep quality more than any single technique.
3. **Customize your fall-asleep time** — If you consistently take 30 minutes to fall asleep, adjust the buffer accordingly. Using an inaccurate fall-asleep time throws off all the cycle calculations.
4. **Avoid screens 30 minutes before bed** — Blue light from phones and laptops suppresses melatonin production and increases fall-asleep latency. Put devices away before your target bedtime for better alignment.
5. **Use the nap calculator wisely** — A 20-minute power nap boosts alertness without entering deep sleep. A 90-minute nap completes one full cycle including REM. Avoid naps longer than 20 minutes but shorter than 90 minutes, as waking mid-cycle during a nap causes significant grogginess.
6. **Track your actual wake-up feelings** — This calculator uses the average 90-minute cycle. Individual cycles can vary from 80 to 120 minutes. If you consistently feel groggy, try shifting your alarm by 10-15 minutes earlier or later to find your personal cycle length.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### How long is one sleep cycle?
One complete sleep cycle lasts approximately 90 minutes on average and consists of four stages. Stage 1 (N1) is light sleep lasting 1-7 minutes as you drift off. Stage 2 (N2) is slightly deeper sleep lasting 10-25 minutes where heart rate and body temperature drop. Stage 3 (N3) is deep sleep (slow-wave sleep) lasting 20-40 minutes, which is the most restorative phase for physical recovery. The fourth stage is REM (Rapid Eye Movement) sleep lasting 10-60 minutes, which is critical for memory consolidation, learning, and emotional regulation. Cycles earlier in the night have more deep sleep, while later cycles have more REM sleep. The 90-minute average is well-supported by research, but individual cycles can range from 80 to 120 minutes.

### Is it better to sleep 6 or 7.5 hours?
From a sleep cycle perspective, 7.5 hours (5 complete cycles) is significantly better than 6 hours (4 complete cycles). Adults need 7-9 hours of sleep for optimal cognitive function, immune health, and emotional well-being. While 6 hours aligned to complete cycles is better than 6.5 hours of mid-cycle interrupted sleep, consistently sleeping only 6 hours leads to cumulative sleep debt that impairs reaction time, decision-making, and long-term health. If you must occasionally choose between 6 and 7.5 hours, always choose 7.5. If you are deciding between 6 hours (4 full cycles) and 7 hours (waking mid-cycle), the 6 hours may leave you feeling more alert in the short term, but you should aim for 7.5 whenever possible.

### Why do I feel tired after 8 hours of sleep?
Feeling tired after 8 hours is almost always caused by waking in the middle of a sleep cycle, specifically during deep sleep (Stage N3). Eight hours does not align neatly with 90-minute cycles: 5 cycles equals 7.5 hours, and 6 cycles equals 9 hours, leaving 8 hours right in the middle of the 6th cycle. This is called sleep inertia — the grogginess and disorientation experienced when waking during deep sleep. The solution is to adjust your alarm to either 7.5 hours (plus your fall-asleep time) or 9 hours rather than an arbitrary 8 hours. Other contributing factors include poor sleep quality from caffeine, alcohol, sleep apnea, or an inconsistent schedule that undermines your circadian rhythm.
