## 🔧 Technical Details

### Processing Technology
- **EMI Formula**: EMI = [P × r × (1 + r)^n] / [(1 + r)^n - 1]
  - P = Principal loan amount (car price - down payment)
  - r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12 / 100)
  - n = Loan tenure in months
- **Total Payment** = EMI × Number of Months
- **Total Interest** = Total Payment - Principal
- Amortization computed month-by-month with reducing balance method
- All computations run in client-side JavaScript

## 📝 Best Practices

1. **Keep EMI below 20% of take-home salary** — Financial advisors recommend car loan EMI should not exceed 20% of your monthly in-hand salary. If you earn Rs. 60,000/month, your car EMI should ideally be under Rs. 12,000.
2. **Pay maximum down payment you can afford** — Higher down payment means smaller loan, lower EMI, and significantly less interest over time. Paying Rs. 3 lakh instead of Rs. 2 lakh down payment on a Rs. 10 lakh car saves over Rs. 50,000 in interest (assuming 10% rate, 5 years).
3. **Choose shortest affordable tenure** — Longer tenures reduce EMI but drastically increase total interest. A 7-year loan at 10% on Rs. 5 lakh costs Rs. 2.8 lakh in interest vs. Rs. 65,000 for a 2-year loan. Pick the shortest tenure you can comfortably afford.
4. **Compare effective interest rate, not just advertised rate** — Include processing fees (typically 0.5-1% of loan amount) and other charges when comparing banks. A 9% loan with Rs. 5,000 processing fee may cost more than a 9.5% loan with zero fees.
5. **Check prepayment and foreclosure charges** — Some banks charge 2-5% penalty for early loan closure. If you plan to prepay the loan using bonus or windfall income, choose a loan with nil or minimal prepayment charges.
6. **Opt for direct bank loan, not dealer financing** — Car dealers often tie up with NBFCs that charge 1-2% higher rates than direct bank loans. Compare dealer offer against your bank's car loan rates.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the eligibility for a car loan in India?
Most banks require: (a) Age 21-65 years, (b) Minimum monthly income Rs. 25,000 (salaried) or Rs. 3 lakh annual ITR (self-employed), (c) CIBIL score 700+, (d) Employment stability (1+ year in current job for salaried, 3+ years in business for self-employed). Banks typically finance 80-90% of the car value — the rest must be paid as down payment.

### How much car loan can I get on a Rs. 50,000 salary?
Using the 20% EMI-to-income rule, you can afford Rs. 10,000 EMI on Rs. 50,000 salary. At 10% interest for 5 years, Rs. 10,000 EMI corresponds to a loan of approximately Rs. 4.7 lakh. Adding 20% down payment (Rs. 1.2 lakh), you can afford a car worth Rs. 5.9 lakh on-road. Banks also consider existing EMIs — if you have other loans, your car loan eligibility reduces.

### Is floating or fixed interest rate better for car loans?
Most car loans in India are fixed-rate or hybrid (fixed for first 2-3 years, floating later). Fixed rates give EMI certainty but are typically 0.25-0.5% higher than floating rates. Floating rates can increase if RBI raises repo rate but also decrease if rates fall. For short tenures (2-3 years), fixed is simpler; for longer tenures (5-7 years), floating may save money if interest rates decline.
