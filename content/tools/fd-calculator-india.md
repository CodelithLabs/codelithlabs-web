# FD Interest Calculator India

Calculate the maturity value of your Fixed Deposit with accurate compounding and TDS deduction. Fixed deposits remain one of the most popular savings instruments in India, offering guaranteed returns with zero market risk. This calculator helps you estimate your FD maturity amount based on the deposit amount, interest rate, tenure, and compounding frequency. It also factors in TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) so you can see your actual post-tax returns and plan your savings accordingly.

## 🚀 Features

- **Maturity Value Calculation** — Compute the exact maturity amount for any deposit amount, rate, and tenure combination
- **Compounding Frequency Options** — Choose between quarterly (most common in India), monthly, half-yearly, or annual compounding
- **TDS Calculation** — Automatically calculates TDS at 10% (or 20% for missing PAN) when interest exceeds Rs. 40,000 per year (Rs. 50,000 for senior citizens)
- **Senior Citizen Rate Toggle** — Switch to see the higher interest rates typically offered to depositors aged 60 and above
- **Bank Rate Comparison** — Pre-loaded interest rates from major Indian banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, PNB, and others) for quick comparison
- **Cumulative vs Non-Cumulative** — Toggle between cumulative FD (interest reinvested) and non-cumulative FD (interest paid out periodically)
- **Indian Number Formatting** — Amounts displayed in lakhs and crores with the rupee symbol

## 📖 How to Use FD Calculator India

1. **Enter Deposit Amount** — Type the amount you want to invest in the fixed deposit (e.g., Rs. 5,00,000).
2. **Set Interest Rate** — Enter the annual interest rate offered by your bank, or select a bank from the dropdown to auto-fill the rate.
3. **Choose Tenure** — Specify the FD duration in years and months (e.g., 5 years or 3 years 6 months).
4. **Select Compounding Frequency** — Choose quarterly (default for most Indian banks), monthly, half-yearly, or annual compounding.
5. **Toggle Senior Citizen** — Enable this to apply the senior citizen premium (typically 0.25% to 0.50% higher).
6. **Review Results** — See the maturity amount, total interest earned, TDS deducted, and net interest received.

### Bank FD Comparison Before Investing
Before locking your money into an FD, compare the maturity values across banks. Even a 0.25% difference in interest rate can mean thousands of rupees difference on a large deposit over several years. Enter the same principal and tenure with different rates to identify the best option.

If your total FD interest across all branches of a bank exceeds Rs. 40,000 in a financial year (Rs. 50,000 for those aged 60+), the bank will deduct TDS at 10%. Retirees who receive a lump-sum provident fund or gratuity payout often park the money in FDs for regular income. FDs work well for short- to medium-term goals with a fixed deadline, such as saving for a vacation, a wedding, or a down payment on a property. Enter your available amount and the target date to see exactly how much the deposit will grow to by then.

## 🔧 Technical Details

### Processing Technology
- Cumulative FD formula: A = P x (1 + r/n)^(n*t), where P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate, n is the compounding frequency, and t is the tenure in years
- Non-cumulative FD: Interest per period = P x r / n (simple interest paid out each period, principal returned at maturity)
- TDS is calculated as 10% of total annual interest when it exceeds the threshold (Rs. 40,000 general / Rs. 50,000 senior citizen)
- All computation runs in client-side JavaScript with no external API calls

### Supported Formats
- Inputs: Deposit amount (no upper limit), annual interest rate (percentage), tenure (years and months), compounding frequency selection
- Outputs: Maturity value, total interest earned, TDS deducted, net interest, effective yield after TDS

## 📝 Best Practices

1. **Compare across banks and NBFCs** — Small finance banks and select NBFCs often offer 0.5% to 1% higher rates than large nationalized banks. Compare the rates using this calculator before committing.
2. **Use the senior citizen benefit** — If you or a family member qualifies for senior citizen rates (age 60+), always ask the bank for the higher rate. The difference of 0.25% to 0.50% adds up significantly on large deposits.
3. **Submit Form 15G/15H to avoid TDS** — If your total taxable income is below the basic exemption limit, submit Form 15G (below 60 years) or Form 15H (60+ years) to the bank at the start of each financial year to prevent unnecessary TDS deduction on FD interest.
4. **Ladder your FDs** — Instead of putting all money into one FD, split it across multiple FDs with staggered maturity dates. This provides liquidity without breaking the entire deposit and losing the interest benefit.
5. **Factor in premature withdrawal penalties** — Most banks charge a 0.5% to 1% penalty on the applicable rate for premature withdrawal. Plan your FD tenure based on when you actually need the money.
6. **Consider tax-saving FDs** — FDs with a 5-year lock-in under Section 80C offer tax deduction on the deposit amount (up to Rs. 1.5 lakh). However, the interest earned is still taxable.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### How is FD interest calculated quarterly?
Most Indian banks compound FD interest on a quarterly basis. The formula is: A = P x (1 + r/4)^(4*t), where P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate as a decimal, and t is the tenure in years. For example, an FD of Rs. 1,00,000 at 7% for 1 year with quarterly compounding would yield: 1,00,000 x (1 + 0.07/4)^4 = Rs. 1,07,186 at maturity. The quarterly compounding means interest is added to the principal every three months, and subsequent interest is calculated on the increased balance.

### What is the TDS rule on FD interest in India?
Banks deduct TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) at 10% on FD interest when the total interest earned from all FDs with the same bank (across all branches) exceeds Rs. 40,000 in a financial year. For senior citizens aged 60 and above, this threshold is Rs. 50,000. If you do not provide your PAN to the bank, TDS is deducted at a higher rate of 20%. You can submit Form 15G (age below 60) or Form 15H (age 60+) to request no TDS deduction if your total income falls below the taxable threshold. TDS deducted can be claimed as a credit when filing your income tax return.

### Do senior citizens get higher FD rates?
Yes, nearly all Indian banks offer a premium interest rate to senior citizens, typically 0.25% to 0.75% higher than the regular rate. Some banks like SBI offer an additional 0.50% for super senior citizens (aged 80+). This higher rate applies automatically when the account holder's age is 60 or above at the time of deposit. The premium makes FDs especially attractive for retirees as a reliable income source. Always verify the current senior citizen rate with your bank, as the premium amount varies by institution and by tenure slab.
