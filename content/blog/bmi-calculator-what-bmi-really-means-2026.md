BMI is a simple screening metric, not a full diagnosis. It helps identify broad risk patterns, but it cannot capture body composition, fitness level, or medical context on its own.

## What BMI Is Good For

- quick population-level screening
- initial personal health awareness
- trend monitoring over time

## What BMI Misses

- muscle vs fat distribution
- age and sex-related context
- metabolic and lifestyle markers

## Better Health Interpretation

Combine BMI with:
- waist measurement
- activity level
- blood markers
- sleep and nutrition patterns

## FAQ

### Is BMI useless?
No. It is useful as a starting signal, not a complete health verdict.

### Can athletes have high BMI but low risk?
Yes, due to muscle mass and body composition differences.

### Should BMI be tracked monthly?
For many users, yes, alongside broader health indicators.

## Final Take

BMI is most valuable when interpreted with context. Use it as one input in a broader health strategy, not a standalone judgment.
