A portfolio growing at 8% during 6% inflation is not an 8% wealth gain in real terms. Investors who ignore inflation often overestimate financial progress.

## Nominal vs Real Return

- **Nominal return:** raw percentage gain
- **Real return:** inflation-adjusted gain

Approximation: real return ≈ nominal return − inflation.

## Why This Changes Planning

Goals like retirement, education, or home purchase depend on future purchasing power, not nominal account values.

## Practical Strategy

- set targets in real terms
- diversify across asset classes
- review inflation assumptions regularly

## FAQ

### Is low inflation always good for investors?
Context matters; policy shifts and growth expectations also affect outcomes.

### Can fixed-income beat inflation?
Sometimes, but not consistently across all cycles.

### Should I rebalance more often during inflation spikes?
Follow policy-driven rebalancing, not panic-driven reactions.

## Final Take

Financial progress should be measured in real purchasing power. Plan with inflation-adjusted assumptions, not headline returns.
