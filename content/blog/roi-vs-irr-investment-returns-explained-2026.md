ROI is simple and useful. IRR is deeper and timing-aware. Confusing the two leads to poor capital decisions.

## ROI: Fast and Intuitive

ROI measures total gain relative to investment cost. Great for quick comparisons.

Limitation: ignores timing of cash flows.

## IRR: Time-Sensitive Return Metric

IRR estimates the discount rate where net present value equals zero.

Strength: captures cash-flow timing.

Limitation: can mislead with unconventional cash-flow patterns or multiple IRRs.

## When to Use Which

- Use ROI for simple, short-horizon decisions
- Use IRR for projects with staged cash flows over time
- Use both when making high-stakes capital allocation decisions

## FAQ

### Can two projects have same ROI but different quality?
Yes, if cash-flow timing differs significantly.

### Is IRR always better?
Not always. It is more informative for time-structured cash flows but also more assumption-sensitive.

### Should I present both ROI and IRR to stakeholders?
Yes, especially for non-trivial projects.

## Final Take

Use ROI for clarity and IRR for depth. Better investment decisions come from matching the metric to the decision context.
